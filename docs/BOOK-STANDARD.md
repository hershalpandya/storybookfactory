# Storybook baseline and review standard

These are the baseline editorial, artwork and print checks for new editions.

## Story and language

- Prepare a separate English story-only text from the supplied sources. Preserve the narrator's voice, events, humor and foreign phrases; remove unrelated conversation and interruptions. Compare inputs, identify disagreement and mark unresolved wording. An automatic transcript alone does not establish what the audio says. Human source review is required before approval.
- Adapt faithfully into the selected language; the French target is natural, grammatically correct, accessible to a beginning CE1 reader. Use clear speakers and explain difficult concepts. Human language review checks fidelity, grammar, vocabulary and reading level.
- The French reading aid keeps full spelling, greys selected silent letters and uses a small arc for selected sounded liaisons. Pronunciation varies with context and region; an unmarked letter is not necessarily a separate sound. Review annotations in each new story, including sounds restored by liaison and ambiguous endings.

## Page and artwork standard

- Use one landscape A4 leaf (297 × 210 mm) per printed side. Prepare single-sided and duplex outputs.
- Use Marelle 2 with proper shaping for French. The landscape master uses nominal 15 pt for body text, 32 pt for the cover title, 18 pt for credits, 14 pt for the date, and 30 pt for the back-cover title. Check print legibility and use suitable fonts and shaping for other scripts. Keep the machine-readable reviewed text alongside the PDFs because shaped text is rasterized.
- Give each story page a distinct scene, varied pacing and useful open drawing space. Place text over quiet, non-action areas with sufficient contrast; do not cover faces or important actions. Avoid crowding, clipped glyphs, cropped colouring outlines and loss of sewing space. Assess artwork anatomy, identities, setting, visual continuity, page-to-text correspondence and the clarity of action; do not assume a generated image is correct.
- Plan a distinct colouring graphic near the bottom of each story page, opposite the physical binding edge. Leave enough space for a child to colour it. The graphic moves to the left on a duplex back. Reserve colouring graphics for story pages.
- Include seasonal details in the scene illustration when they belong there. Keep decorative overlays clear of the text and colouring space.

## Printing

- Keep reading, regular single-sided and regular duplex PDFs distinct. On regular print PDFs, three small sewing marks sit 9 mm from the binding edge at 22%, 50% and 78% of page height. Single-sided pages use the left edge; duplex backs use the right edge in the PDF, aligning with the front's left physical edge on short-edge flip. Reading pages have no sewing marks.
- Print one page per A4 side, landscape, at actual size, with booklet and multiple-up disabled; duplex uses short-edge flip. Verify the feed direction and alignment on one sheet before the full run, especially for manual duplex.
- Printer-safe single-sided and duplex files are separate derivatives: content is proportionally centered into an 8 mm minimum safe area, the reading-mark greys in text-line images are darkened, and the three binding marks are redrawn 10.5 mm from the appropriate edge. The landscape height limits scale to approximately 92.38%, so nominal 15 pt story text becomes approximately 13.86 pt before any printer scaling. An 8 mm content target cannot guarantee that every printer can print to it; verify on the actual device. The safe files preserve the regular files.

## Required review gates and limits

1. Compare source texts and the story-only transcription; record uncertainties, obtain human source approval, and do not claim audio verification without listening and checking.
2. Review adaptation for fidelity, language and age level; separately review each pronunciation annotation in context, with qualified human review when accuracy matters.
3. Review each scene and colouring graphic for story alignment, anatomy, recognizable identities, continuity, size and clear outlines. Family-derived images and likenesses require separate privacy and rights clearance before any public use.
4. Inspect every page and both sides of each output at final size for text fit, shaping, legibility, visual overlap, open space, edge placement, page order and sewing marks. Inspect regular and printer-safe outputs independently, including odd-page behavior in new books.
5. Perform a physical one-sheet duplex check followed by a printed and sewn proof; record actual results.
